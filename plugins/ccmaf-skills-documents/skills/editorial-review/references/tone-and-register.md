# Tone and Register

Tone is judged against the reader and the purpose, never against the
reviewer's taste. A tone note is only useful if it names the target, the
mismatch, and an example of what would fit.

## Establish the target first

From the brief, the house voice guide, or the author:

- **Reader:** who, how senior, how familiar, how sympathetic.
- **Purpose:** inform, persuade, instruct, apologise, refuse, reassure.
- **Relationship:** internal colleague, customer, regulator, partner,
  public.
- **Register:** formal (tenders, policies, regulator letters), professional
  (most workplace documents), conversational (internal updates, blogs).
  `uncanny`'s `references/registers.md` describes how register changes
  what counts as good prose; use the same register names.

If none of these is known, ask, or state the assumption at the top of the
tone notes.

## What to check

- **Consistency of register.** The commonest problem in multi-author or
  assembled documents: a formal tender with one section in chatty second
  person, or a friendly newsletter with a paragraph lifted from a policy.
- **Fit to purpose.** Bad news delivered with cheerful framing; a request
  phrased so politely the ask disappears; an instruction phrased as a
  suggestion ("you might want to") when it is mandatory.
- **Fit to relationship.** Over-familiarity with a regulator;
  condescension to customers ("simply", "obviously", "as everyone
  knows"); defensive or blaming language in an apology.
- **Certainty.** Hedging that undermines claims the author can stand
  behind; confident language for claims that are actually uncertain. Tie
  to the fact-check: an UNVERIFIABLE claim stated as certain is both a
  tone and a fact issue.
- **Clarity as tone.** Jargon, undefined acronyms and abstraction read as
  distance or evasion to a lay reader.
- **Inclusive and respectful language.** See below.
- **AI-generated texture.** Inflated significance, throat-clearing and
  formulaic structure change how a reader trusts the text. Note suspected
  AI tells and route detection and fixing to `uncanny` (audit mode for a
  full list).

## Inclusive and respectful language

Check for, and suggest alternatives to:

- gendered defaults where gender is unknown or irrelevant ("chairman",
  "he" for an unspecified person): use the role or "they";
- language that defines people by a condition or characteristic where it
  is not relevant; follow the preferences of the group described or the
  house guide;
- idioms and cultural references that will not travel to an
  international reader;
- age, ability or background assumptions ("even a grandparent could do
  this").

Suggest, do not lecture: one-line rationale per note, and defer to an
organisational guide where one exists. Quoted material is not altered;
if a quote contains problematic language, query whether to keep it.

## Writing actionable tone notes

Weak:

> The tone here is a bit off.

Strong:

> Section 4, para 2: shifts to second person and contractions ("you'll
> find we've…"); the rest of the tender is third person and formal, and
> the house guide asks for no contractions. Suggest: "The Trust has…".

A tone note contains: location, what the text does, what the target is
(and where that target comes from), and one example of a fix. Offer the
example as a suggestion, not a replacement of the whole passage.

## Tone notes are not rewrites

- Mark the passages; do not recast the document in the reviewer's voice.
- Where the fix is mechanical (removing contractions to meet a formal
  house rule), suggest the edits as tracked changes.
- Where the fix needs the author's judgement (softening a refusal,
  repositioning bad news), write a query, not an edit.
- If the whole document misses its tone, say so once in the report with
  two or three representative examples, and recommend a redraft
  (`document-authoring`), rather than marking every sentence.

## Quick register signals

| Signal | Formal | Professional | Conversational |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contractions | Avoid | Sparingly | Normal |
| Second person ("you") | Avoid, except instructions | Fine for instructions | Normal |
| First person plural ("we") | Organisational "we" acceptable | Normal | Normal |
| Sentence fragments | Avoid | Rare | Acceptable |
| Humour | Avoid | Rare, never at anyone's expense | Acceptable |
| Exclamation marks | Avoid | Avoid | Sparingly |

These are defaults; the house guide overrides them.
