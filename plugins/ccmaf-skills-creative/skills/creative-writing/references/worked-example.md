# Worked example: a short-story opening

Fictional writer, fictional story. The writer shares the opening of a
literary short story (target about 5,000 words) and says: "Something's off
with the start but I can't tell what. Be honest." That is a request for
**critic** mode, with permission to show an example edit if useful.

## The draft (writer's v01, extract)

> The rain had been falling all morning over the small coastal town,
> grey and relentless. Maeve woke up late, her head heavy. She
> looked out at the harbour and felt a deep sadness wash over her. It had
> been three years since her brother Colm had drowned, and today was the
> anniversary. She was dreading going to the pier where it had happened.
>
> She got dressed slowly. Her mother would be waiting at the church, and
> Maeve knew she would have to face her questions about why she hadn't come
> home for the last two anniversaries. Maeve felt guilty about that. She
> also felt angry, because her mother had always loved Colm more.
>
> At the pier, old Tom Hennessy was mending nets. "Maeve," he said warmly.
> "It's good to see you home, love. Your mother's missed you terribly."
>
> "I know," Maeve said sadly. "I know she has."

## 1. Diagnosis (one paragraph to the writer)

> This is a story about a woman coming home to a grief she has avoided and
> a mother she resents; the central question seems to be whether Maeve and
> her mother can face Colm's death together. The material is strong: the
> anniversary, the pier, the two missed years. The opening holds the reader
> at arm's length because it tells us every feeling Maeve has before we
> have seen her do anything, and it uses up its best tension (the missed
> anniversaries, the favouritism) as summary in paragraph two, so the
> meeting with her mother has nothing left to reveal.

## 2. What works

- The pier as the place of the drowning and the place she must walk to: a
  strong, concrete destination.
- Tom mending nets: an ordinary life going on at the scene of the death.
- "Your mother's missed you terribly" lands as quiet pressure. Keep it.

## 3. Prioritised notes

1. **Story: the conflict with the mother is summarised, not dramatised.**
   Paragraph two tells us the guilt, the anger and the favouritism. These
   are the story's engine; let them surface in scene, ideally in the first
   exchange with the mother, so the reader discovers them. Options: cut
   paragraph two entirely, or reduce it to one concrete hint (an unopened
   letter from her mother on the dresser).
2. **Scene: start later.** Weather and waking up delay the pressure. The
   story's pressure begins when she is walking to the pier, or standing on
   it. Option: open on the pier with Tom.
3. **Line: emotions labelled.** "felt a deep sadness wash over her", "felt
   guilty", "felt angry", "said sadly", "said warmly". Each labels what the
   moment could show. Option: keep one physical or behavioural detail per
   feeling and cut the label.
4. **Query:** is Tom's affection meant to contrast with the mother's
   coldness, or to add pressure? His line currently does the latter well.

Smaller notes held back until the structure is settled, as agreed.

## 4. Example edit (offered, clearly marked as one option)

The writer asked to see notes 2 and 3 applied to show the direction. The
edit keeps their names, setting, plain diction, past tense and close third,
and their line from Tom.

> Old Tom Hennessy was mending nets at the end of the pier, where the
> railing was new. Maeve stopped short of it.
>
> "Maeve." He set the needle down. "It's good to see you home, love. Your
> mother's missed you terribly."
>
> "I know." She looked at the new railing, and then at the water, which
> was only water. "I know she has."

What changed and why, for the writer:

- Opens on the pier (note 2); the weather and waking up are gone.
- "Where the railing was new" implies the accident without stating it; the
  reader will learn the rest in the scene with the mother.
- The labelled feelings are replaced by her stopping short and looking at
  the railing (note 3).
- Paragraph two's backstory is held back for the church scene (note 1).
- "The water, which was only water" is the assistant's addition, flagged as
  such: cut it if it does not sound like the writer.
