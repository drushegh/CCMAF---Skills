# Synthesis

Synthesis answers the question across sources. It is organised by claim or
theme, never as "Source A says… Source B says…" in sequence (that is an
annotated bibliography, which is fine when asked for, but is not synthesis).

## The synthesis matrix

Build a grid before writing: sources down the side, the sub-questions or
themes across the top, each cell a claim ID or "silent".

```markdown
| Source | Effect size? | Mechanism? | Cost? | Date range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| harlow2025grid | CL-01 (30 % fewer events) | CL-03 storage response speed | silent | 2022–24 |
| okafor2026grid | CL-01 contra (≈ 10 %) | CL-05 demand response | CL-08 | 2021–24 |
| regulator2026annual | CL-01 (fewer events, no size) | silent | CL-09 | 2024 |
```

The matrix shows at a glance where sources agree, conflict, or are silent,
and which themes rest on one source.

## Writing by theme

For each theme:

1. **State the best-supported position** with its claims cited.
2. **State the disagreement**, if any, and attribute it.
3. **Explain the disagreement where the sources allow:** different time
   periods, populations, methods, definitions, or interests (funding,
   advocacy). Mark this explanation `[me]` unless a source makes it.
4. **Say how confident the position is** and why.

Prefer "Two of three sources find X [refs]; the third, which controls for
Y, finds a smaller effect [ref]" over "Research shows X".

## Handling conflict

- Do not average numbers from incompatible methods.
- Do not pick the source you prefer without saying why.
- Newer is not automatically better; check whether the newer source
  addresses the older one.
- Note when one side has a stake in the answer, but do not dismiss evidence
  on that basis alone; check the method.

## Independence and the echo problem

Before counting sources as corroboration, ask whether they share an origin:
the same dataset, the same press release, the same expert, one citing the
other. Trace to the original and count it once.

## Gaps and limits

End each synthesis with:

- **What the sources do not cover** (time periods, populations, angles).
- **What you could not access** (paywalled, unavailable).
- **What would change the conclusion** (a specific study, newer data).
- **As-of date.**

## From notes to a deliverable

- Draft from the synthesis notes, not from memory of the sources.
- Keep claim IDs in the draft until the final pass, then convert to the
  required citation format (footnotes, links, or a reference style via
  `academic-research`).
- Run the traceability audit (`claim-traceability.md`) on the draft.
- Document structure and prose: `document-authoring`, `technical-writing`,
  `editorial-review`, `uncanny`.

## Living syntheses

For research that continues over weeks:

- Date each update in the synthesis note.
- When a new source changes a position, update the claim ledger first, then
  the synthesis, and add a short dated change line ("2026-11-02: CL-01
  downgraded to contested after okafor2026grid").
- Keep superseded positions visible in the change history rather than
  rewriting as if the conclusion was always the current one.

## Pitfalls

- Source-by-source summaries presented as synthesis.
- False balance: presenting a fringe source as equal to a strong body of
  evidence. Weight by quality, and say how you weighted.
- False consensus: smoothing over a real disagreement.
- Conclusions stronger than any single source supports.
- Dropping the gaps section because it looks weak. It is what makes the rest
  trustworthy.
